## The Portability Gap: On the Non-Reproducibility of Distributed Database Latency Benchmarks

Latency measurements occupy a privileged position in the evaluation of distributed database systems. Tail latency figures reported in conference papers, vendor white papers, and public benchmark repositories are routinely treated as portable properties of a system, and are used to justify architectural claims and procurement decisions alike. This paper argues that such figures are not reproducible across deployment environments, and that the failure is structural rather than a consequence of methodological carelessness in individual studies.

We develop the argument in three parts. First, we characterise the environmental surface on which a distributed database's latency distribution depends: virtualisation layer and hypervisor scheduling, network topology and cross-zone path variability, storage device class and its garbage-collection behaviour, kernel and runtime versions, co-tenancy on shared hardware, and the placement decisions of the cloud provider's control plane. Most of these are neither controlled nor disclosed in published evaluations, and several are not observable to the experimenter at all. Second, we show that these factors interact with the system under test rather than contributing an additive offset: replication protocols, timeout and retry policies, and adaptive load balancing amplify environmental variance in ways that depend on the protocol's own design, so that a system's relative ranking may invert under a change of substrate. Third, we examine the reporting conventions that obscure this dependence, including the summarisation of distributions by a small number of percentiles, the omission of run-to-run variance, and the practice of tuning configurations against the very environment in which results are collected.

We conclude that latency claims should be understood as claims about a system-environment pair rather than about a system, and we propose reporting practices that make the environmental envelope explicit: disclosure of substrate characteristics, repeated trials across distinct deployments, and the publication of full distributions alongside the artefacts required to re-run them.
